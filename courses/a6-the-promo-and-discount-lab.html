The promo and discount lab

In November 2017 everything on Olist sold more: Black Friday fell on the 24th. Some sellers had cut their prices and some had not. A promo report that credits the whole November jump to the discounts says the cuts lifted sales by three quarters. Tonight climbs five rungs on the real rows, from that naive number to a comparison group, the month after, and the question that matters: did the discount pay? Then it counts the crowd as discount lift on purpose, and tries to break the result by shuffling which products were cut.

Where we start

Analyst sessions 1 to 5 built the parts: the price table, willingness to pay, a decision across an elasticity range, promo lift with its baseline and the month after, and the price-volume-mix bridge. Tonight they meet on one lab. What is real: monthly units and mean item prices for every Olist product in the panel built by the Economics course, July 2017 to January 2018, delivered orders, in Brazilian reais. What is assumed: the cost ratio, because Olist publishes no costs; you set it with a slider. What it is not: a randomised test. Sellers chose which products to cut.

Part 1 · covers what the lab scores

Five rungs, one question: what did the discount do?

7 min live

Every rung asks the same question with one more thing taken out of the answer. Rung 1 takes nothing out. Rung 2 looks only at products that cut price. Rung 3 subtracts what products that held price did in the same month. Rung 4 adds December, where pull-forward would show as a dip. Rung 5 turns units into money at a cost ratio you choose.

LiveThe five rungs, defined once4 min▶

Rung 1, whole panel. November units over October units for every product, minus one. What a promo report that ignores the calendar would print.

Rung 2, cut group alone. The same for the 29 products whose November mean price was more than 3% below October's. Closer to the discount, still riding the season.

Rung 3, minus the held group. The 54 products that held price within 3% show what the season did without a cut. Their growth, applied to the cut group's October, is the cut group's expected November; anything above it is credited to the cut. This is a difference-in-differences comparison: Experimentation, difference-in-differences ↗ teaches the method and its parallel-trends assumption.

Rung 4, the month after. The same comparison for December. If the discount only pulled purchases forward, December shows a dip below expected. Add the two months.

Rung 5, did it pay? Value the extra units at October prices, compare with the break-even rise d / (m - d) from Business session 1, and print profit against no discount at the assumed cost ratio.

Self-studyWhy these rows, and what they cannot say3 min▶

Public promotion data with prices, units and a comparison group is rare. The Olist panel (Brazilian E-Commerce Public Dataset by Olist, Kaggle, CC BY-NC-SA 4.0) was built by the Economics course to estimate elasticity; this lab reuses the same file and never refits elasticity. Three limits are printed on the widget. The grain is monthly, so Black Friday itself is one day inside a month row. Sellers chose which products to cut, so the comparison is a design, not a randomised test (Experimentation, when you can't randomize ↗). And there are no costs, so every margin is at an assumed cost ratio.

Build-along · the lab

Run the promo and discount lab

★ 22 min · everyone builds

Work the rungs in order and say out loud what changed. Then move the cost ratio, try a narrower scope, switch on the anti-lever, and press the break button. Every number below is recomputed from the rows in your browser.

Read it in this order. The n line first: how many products sit behind the number. Then the verdict. Then the tiles. A lift with 8 products behind it and a lift with 80 are not the same claim, which is why the lab refuses to compute under its floor.

Step 1 · Rung 1, the whole panel. Select it and read the verdict. This is the number a promo report that credits everything to the discount would print.

★ Step 1 · what the lab printsRung 1: November units +74.7% on October across the panel (877 to 1,532). Every product rode the season.

Step 2 · Rungs 2 and 3. The cut group alone rose more than the panel. Then subtract the held group.

★ Step 2 · cut alone, then minus heldRung 2: the cut group rose +111.0% (310 to 654 units): 344 extra units, but held products rose too. Rung 3: cut +111.0% minus held +65.5% leaves +45.5 points: 141 extra units in November.

Step 3 · Rung 4, the month after. A dip in December would be pull-forward. Read the sign.

★ Step 3 · DecemberRung 4: December +23 units against the held group, so 164 extra units over November and December. no dip at monthly grain for the whole panel; now set Scope to "Home and garden (grouped)": Rung 4: December -16.6 units against the held group, so 30.5 extra units over November and December.

Step 4 · Rung 5, did it pay? Set Scope back to All products. Read rung 5 at the default 60% cost ratio, then slide to 50%.

★ Step 4 · money, at an assumed costRung 5: discount depth 7.9% at an assumed 60.0% cost ratio needs 24.6% more volume; measured 20.4%. It did not pay: -R$ 1,058.10. at 50%: ... needs 18.7% more volume; measured 20.4%. It paid: +R$ 550.78.

Step 5 · The anti-lever, then the floor. Select "Count the Black Friday crowd as discount lift". Then set Scope to garden_tools only.

★ Step 5 · two ways to fool yourselfAnti-lever: discount depth 7.9% at an assumed 60.0% cost ratio needs 24.6% more volume; measured 70.5%. It paid: +R$ 8,210.60. Counting the crowd credits 422 extra units where the comparison group allows 164: overstated by 258 units, 2.57 times the honest figure. Refused: 7 cut and 4 held products is under the floor of 8 each. Widen the scope.

Step 6 · Break it. Scope All products, rung 3, then press "Break it: shuffle which products were cut". Read where the real gap sits among 200 shuffles.

★ Step 6 · the break buttonReal gap +45.5 points sits above 186 of 200 shuffles; 14 shuffles reach it. Middle 90% of shuffles: -53.9 to +50.2 points. At this n the gap is inside the shuffle spread: the cut-group advantage is not separable from which products happened to be cut.
Part 2 · covers the anti-lever

Counting the crowd: the discount that always pays

6 min live

Drop the comparison group and October becomes the baseline for both months. The cut group's 654 November units against its 310 in October look like 344 units of discount lift, and December adds 78 more. The held group says most of that is the season: it rose 65.5% without any cut. Counting the crowd overstates the lift 2.57 times, and the same rows that did not pay at a 60% cost ratio now "paid" R$ 8,210.60.

LiveWhy the anti-lever is not a straw man3 min▶

It is the default report. "Units during the promotion against the month before" is how many promo dashboards are built, because it needs no second group. In a flat month it is roughly right. In November it credits the whole season to the discount.

It flips the decision. At the default 60% cost ratio, the comparison says the discount lost R$ 1,058.10 over two months. The crowd-counted version says it made R$ 8,210.60. Same rows, same prices, opposite recommendation for next year.

Honest nuance. The held group is not a perfect stand-in either: products that held price may differ from products that were cut (bigger sellers, different categories). That is the parallel-trends assumption, and Part 3 tests one version of it.

Self-studyPull-forward and carryover2 min▶

A discount that only moves purchases earlier leaves a dip after it: December below what the held group predicts. For all products, December sits 23 units above the comparison, so at monthly grain there is no dip, if anything a small carryover. For the Home and garden scope it sits 16.6 below, a dip. Monthly data blurs this: a dip in the first week of December and a recovery by Christmas net out inside one row. Weekly or daily data would answer what this panel cannot.

Real world

The promotion that paid every year. Kettle's November tin promotion had a dashboard showing a 60% lift every year, measured against October. When the data team added the tins that were not on promotion as a comparison, most of the lift turned out to be gifting season: unpromoted tins rose almost as much. The promotion continued, but at half the depth. Constructed Kettle & Co example; the real Olist rows above show the same shape.

Part 3 · covers the placebo break button

Break it: is the gap more than which products were cut?

6 min live

The comparison says cut products grew 45.5 points more than held ones. Would a random split of the same 83 products into 29 and 54 give a gap that big? The break button answers by shuffling the labels 200 times with a fixed seed. If the real gap sits far outside the shuffles, the split is doing more than chance. Here it does not: 14 of 200 shuffles reach it.

LiveWhat the break button proves, and what it does not3 min▶

It tests the split, not the cause. Each shuffle keeps the 83 products and their real units and only reassigns the label "cut". Product sales are lumpy: a few products that tripled in November can land in either group by chance. When 14 of 200 random splits produce a gap as large as the real one, the real 45.5 points cannot be told apart from that lumpiness at this n.

It does not say the discount did nothing. It says these rows cannot separate it from noise. The honest report is a range and a caveat, not a zero and not a 45.5.

The forking path. At a 5% threshold the real gap of 72.8 points beats 198 of 200 shuffles. Choose the threshold before you look. Trying thresholds until the test passes turns a placebo check into a search for a story.

Self-studyHow to get a cleaner answer next time2 min▶

Randomise. Pick the products, stores or customers that get the discount by a random draw, hold the rest back, and size the test before it starts: Experimentation, run an A/B test ↗. Use weekly data so the promotion and the weeks after it are separate rows. Record the cost per unit at the time, so rung 5 needs no assumption. If none of that is possible, the comparison group plus a placebo is the minimum, reported with its n.

Real world

The honest sentence. Here is what the lab supports, on real rows at the default settings: "In November 2017 the 29 Olist products that cut price more than 3% sold 141 more units than the 54 that held would predict, 164 over two months; on 83 products that gap is inside the range random splits produce; at an assumed 60% cost ratio it did not pay, at 50% it did." Every clause is a line the lab prints. That is longer than "the discount lifted sales 75%", and it is the one you can defend.

Homework

Before you sign the next promotion

45 min
  1. Run the ladder on your own last promotion. Find a group of products, stores or weeks that did not get it, and compute rungs 1 to 4 with their units.
  2. Get the real cost ratio for the promoted products from finance and compute rung 5 at that number and at 10 points either side.
  3. Shuffle your own labels 200 times with a fixed seed and report where the real gap sits.
  4. Write the honest sentence for your promotion in the shape of Part 3's example, every clause backed by a number you computed.
Check yourself

Three questions

Clear all three and Analyst session 6 gets stamped in your passport.

1 · The cut products rose 111.0% in November and the held products 65.5%. What does rung 3 credit to the discount?

The held group shows what the season did without a cut. Only the part above it is credited, and only under the parallel-trends assumption.

2 · With the anti-lever on, the same rows say the discount "paid" R$ 8,210.60 at a 60% cost ratio instead of losing R$ 1,058.10. Why?

Without a comparison group the season is credited to the discount, the measured volume rise jumps from 20.4% to 70.5%, and the break-even test is passed by the calendar.

3 · The real gap of +45.5 points sits above 186 of 200 shuffles; 14 reach it. What is the honest reading?

A placebo that reaches the real gap one time in fourteen means "not separable from chance at this n", not "zero". D is the forking path: choose the threshold before you look.
